Opening

Test cricket is the longest argument in sport. It began in 1877 and has been revised, challenged, mourned and resurrected in every decade since. What makes this moment different is not the pace at which England are scoring runs — it is the conviction with which they have decided that the game's future depends on making it worth watching again.

We don't play Test cricket to survive. We play it to find out what we're made of — and to make it worth two thousand people getting up at 3am on the other side of the world.

Ben Stokes, England Test Captain

02

Bazball

The word is a tabloid coinage that became a philosophy. Under Brendon McCullum and Ben Stokes, England's Test team adopted a set of principles that sound simple and are extraordinarily difficult to execute: bat without fear, set the pace, never allow the opposition to dictate terms.

◆

Intent

The first principle is non-negotiable: every ball is there to be scored off. This does not mean recklessness — it means that defence is a tactical choice, not a default. England's average first-session scoring rate since 2022 is 4.3 runs per over, compared to a global Test average of 3.1.

◆

Trust

McCullum's signal achievement has been convincing players that failure is not career-ending. England's top-order batters average more than 50 balls faced before they are dismissed — a higher average than at any point in the previous decade — but they are also dismissed more often for scores below 20. The trust cuts both ways.

◆

The Chases

The statistical hallmark of the Stokes era is England's fourth-innings record. They have successfully chased 21 targets in 30 attempts since June 2022 — a conversion rate of 70%, compared to a global Test average of 41%. Three of those chases exceeded 350 runs, a figure previously achieved only once in Test history.

◆

The Limits

Bazball has not been universally successful. On rank-turning pitches in South Asia, the approach requires modification that has not always been convincingly made. The 2025 tour of India — lost 3–1 — exposed the limits of aggressive intent against world-class spin on home pitches. The question of adaptability remains open.

There is no series in world cricket that carries this weight. We never stop being aware of it, and we never stop pretending that we aren't.

Joe Root, England batter

The Ashes 2025

England retained the Ashes in 2025, winning the series 3–1 with a match to spare. It was the first time England had retained the urn in successive series since 1986–87. These are the five Tests that defined the summer.

England won

First Test · Headingley · England won by 9 wickets

England's Ollie Pope scored 196 on the first day — the highest score by an England batter at Headingley since Len Hutton's 202 in 1939. Australia were bowled out for 147 in their second innings as Mark Wood took 5–32 at 95mph.

Second Test · Lord's · Australia won by 43 runs

Australia's Pat Cummins was magnificent in both innings, taking nine wickets in the match. England, set 271 to win, were bowled out for 227 in the final session as Nathan Lyon turned the ball sharply on a deteriorating surface.

The Women's Game

England Women have won successive Ashes series, a World T20 title and a first women's Test at Lord's in forty years. They are no longer the secondary story. They are, in many respects, the more interesting one.

England Women played four Test matches in 2025 — more than in any year since 1998. The return of multi-Test series, including the first women's Test at Lord's since 1984, represents a structural commitment to the long form of the women's game that was previously absent. Attendance at the Lord's Test was 22,000 across four days.

England Women won the ICC T20 World Cup in Bangladesh in October 2025, defeating Australia in the final by 8 runs. It was England's first global women's title since the 2009 T20 World Cup. Sophie Ecclestone was named Player of the Tournament after taking 17 wickets at an average of 8.2.

Recreational women's and girls' cricket participation reached 460,000 registered players in 2025 — a 34% increase since 2022. The ECB's Dynamos Cricket programme now reaches 180,000 girls aged 8–11 annually. The pipeline from grassroots to county to international is, for the first time, genuinely continuous.

07

The County Game

The County Championship is the engine room of English cricket. It is also its most contested institution — perpetually debated, restructured and defended. The 2025 season produced the closest title race in twenty years.

Surrey won the County Championship for the third time in four seasons, confirming their position as the dominant county side of the mid-2020s. Their success has been built on an exceptional pace attack — Atkinson, Overton and Clarke took 187 Championship wickets between them — allied to the reliable batting of Rory Burns, who scored 1,412 runs in the season at an average of 58.

We don't talk about legacy at Surrey. We talk about what we're doing tomorrow.

Gareth Batty, Surrey head coach
14 Championship wins in 2025
1,412 Rory Burns: runs in the Championship season

Lancashire pushed Surrey to the final week of the season, ultimately finishing four points behind. Their top-order batting — anchored by Keaton Jennings and the emerging Josh Bohannon — was the strongest in the division. Lancashire's failure to win the Championship has become one of county cricket's recurring narratives; they have finished second or third in six of the past ten seasons.

4 Points separating Surrey and Lancashire at the final reckoning

Derbyshire's promotion from Division Two was the story of the domestic season. Under new head coach Andy Hurry, they developed three England-qualified seamers under 23 and won nine of their fourteen Championship matches. Their return to the top flight after four seasons away was comfortable and convincing.

Voices of the Game

The people who shaped English cricket in 2025.

Ben Stokes

England Test Captain

In three years as captain, Ben Stokes has transformed England's Test team from a demoralised group finishing the 2021–22 Ashes without a win to the most watchable Test side in the world. His own performances — 847 runs and 34 wickets in 2025 — suggest that the captaincy has liberated rather than burdened him.

Player profile

Brendon McCullum

England Test Head Coach

The New Zealander brought to English cricket something it had spent thirty years trying to manufacture: genuine fearlessness. His coaching philosophy — outlined in the now-famous 'Headingley meeting' of June 2022 — has become a case study in sports leadership internationally.

Coaching profile

Nat Sciver-Brunt

England Women's Captain

The finest all-round cricketer England have produced in a generation, Nat Sciver-Brunt leads the women's team with the same combination of authority and warmth that defines her batting. Her return from a period of mental health leave in 2023 has been one of sport's more remarkable comebacks.
